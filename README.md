# osTicket - Ticket Lifecycle: Intake Through Resolution

This project documents the lifecycle of a help desk ticket in **osTicket**, from initial intake through assignment, troubleshooting, communication, and resolution. The lab is designed to demonstrate the workflow and documentation habits used in a real IT support environment.

---

## Project Objective

The goal of this lab is to practice working tickets the way a help desk technician would in a production environment: review the user's issue, determine priority and ownership, communicate clearly, document troubleshooting, apply or recommend a resolution, and verify the outcome before closing the ticket.

## Environments and Technologies Used

- Microsoft Azure (Virtual Machines / Compute)
- osTicket
- Windows 10
- Internet Information Services (IIS)
- Remote Desktop

## Ticket Lifecycle Stages

1. **Intake** - Review the user's request and gather the information needed to understand the issue.
2. **Assignment and Communication** - Route the ticket to the appropriate technician or team and communicate with the user as needed.
3. **Troubleshooting** - Investigate the issue, document findings, and work toward a resolution.
4. **Resolution** - Apply the fix, document what resolved the issue, verify service is restored, and close the ticket appropriately.

---

## Help Desk Workflow

### 1. Intake and Initial Review

When a ticket enters the queue, the first step is to read the user's description carefully and identify the affected user, device or service, business impact, symptoms, and any recent changes that may be related to the problem.

A strong initial review helps prevent unnecessary troubleshooting and gives the technician enough context to decide what should happen next.

### 2. Assignment and Communication

After the issue is understood, the ticket can be assigned to the appropriate technician or support team. Clear communication is important throughout the process so the user knows the issue is being worked and the next technician can understand what has already been attempted.

Internal notes should document technical findings, troubleshooting performed, and any information that would help another technician continue the work without starting over.

### 3. Troubleshooting the Issue

Troubleshooting should follow a structured process rather than immediately changing settings. The technician confirms the symptoms, checks the most likely causes, reviews relevant system or network information, tests one change at a time, and records the results.

This approach makes the work easier to reproduce, escalate, and audit.

### 4. Resolution and Verification

A ticket should not be considered complete simply because a change was made. The technician should confirm that the original problem is resolved, document the successful fix, communicate the result to the user, and record any useful follow-up information before closing the ticket.

## Key Takeaway

A well-managed ticket creates a clear record of the problem, the troubleshooting performed, the resolution, and the final verification. That documentation allows support teams to work efficiently, hand tickets between technicians, and provide a consistent experience to users.
